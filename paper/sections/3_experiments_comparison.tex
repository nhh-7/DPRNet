\subsection{Comparison with state-of-the-art}
\label{sec:main_comparison}

We compare DPRNet against eight representative lightweight SR methods spanning
CNN-based designs (CARN~\cite{ahn2018carn}, IMDN~\cite{hui2019imdn},
RFDN~\cite{liu2020rfdn}, RLFN~\cite{kong2022rlfn}) and Transformer/routing-based
designs (SwinIR-light~\cite{liang2021swinir}, ELAN-light~\cite{zhang2022elan},
SRFormer-light~\cite{zhou2023srformer}, and the most direct predecessor
CATANet~\cite{liu2025catanet}). Competitor numbers are cited verbatim from the
original papers (see the notes of Table~\ref{tab:main_comparison}); DPRNet uses a
single unified checkpoint per scale, reported across all five benchmarks without
per-dataset selection.

\paragraph{Overall accuracy.}
Table~\ref{tab:main_comparison} shows that DPRNet and CATANet form the top tier at
all three scales, both clearly ahead of the remaining seven methods. For example,
at $\times4$ DPRNet reaches $32.58$/$0.8998$\,dB on Set5 and $27.76$/$0.7434$ on
BSD100, exceeding the strongest non-routing competitor SRFormer-light
($32.51$/$0.8988$ and $27.73$/$0.7422$) while using a comparable parameter budget.
The consistent advantage of the two content-routing methods over window- and
distillation-based baselines supports the value of content-aware token
aggregation in the lightweight regime.

\paragraph{Comparison with CATANet.}
Against CATANet---the strongest baseline and the method DPRNet differs from only in
the routing module---DPRNet is on par, trading the lead across benchmarks rather
than dominating. DPRNet attains the best BSD100 results at both $\times3$
($29.29$/$0.8103$ vs.\ $29.28$/$0.8101$) and $\times4$ ($27.76$/$0.7434$ vs.\
$27.75$/$0.7427$), the best Manga109 at $\times2$ ($39.38$/$0.9787$ vs.\
$39.37$/$0.9784$), and matches CATANet on Set5 at $\times4$ (both $32.58$/$0.8998$).
On Urban100 it is a close second at every scale (e.g.\ $26.82$ vs.\ $26.87$ at
$\times4$), and similarly within $0.01$--$0.08$\,dB on Set5/Set14. These differences
are small and benchmark-dependent: replacing the EMA-center routing of TAB with the
dynamic prototype routing of DPR preserves CATANet-level reconstruction quality
while changing the routing mechanism to the interpretable, confidence-aware
formulation analyzed in Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation} and Sec.~\ref{sec:routing_analysis}.

\paragraph{Efficiency.}
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} reports the efficiency profile at $\times4$. DPRNet has
$660$K parameters and $45.77$\,G Multi-Adds for a fixed $\approx720{\times}1280$
output, keeping it within the lightweight bracket alongside the compared methods
while delivering top-tier accuracy. DPRNet's parameter count is modestly higher than
CATANet's ($660$K vs.\ $535$K at $\times4$), reflecting the learnable query bank and
router projections of DPR; the per-scale profile is given in
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_profile}. A complete Multi-Adds/latency comparison against
all competitors under our measurement protocol is left for the final version, as
those numbers are not yet collected under a unified setting.
