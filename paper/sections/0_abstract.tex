\begin{abstract}
Lightweight single image super-resolution (SR) seeks a favorable trade-off between
reconstruction quality and model cost on resource-constrained platforms. Content-aware
routing improves this trade-off by grouping tokens according to semantics rather than
spatial position, yet its prevailing realization---the Token Aggregation Block (TAB) in
CATANet---uses cross-batch exponential-moving-average routing centers, discards the
confidence of token-to-center assignments, and couples prototype generation with
confirmation. We propose \emph{Dynamic Prototype Routing} (DPR), a drop-in replacement
for TAB that preserves the backbone, tensor interface, and lightweight budget. DPR
generates semantic prototypes from the current tokens through soft assignment, confirms
them with a learnable query bank decoupled from generation, matches tokens back using a
learnable temperature, and carries per-token confidence through ordering, gating, and a
soft fallback. A usage-balancing regularizer further keeps prototype use spread out. The
resulting network, DPRNet, is trained on DIV2K and evaluated on five standard benchmarks
at $\times2/\times3/\times4$. DPRNet matches or slightly surpasses the strongest
content-routing baseline across benchmarks at a comparable parameter and computation
budget. At $\times4$, for example, it obtains $26.89$\,dB on Urban100 with
$52.14$\,G Multi-Adds, fewer than the lightweight Transformer baselines SwinIR-light and
SRFormer-light, while exposing an interpretable, confidence-aware routing mechanism with
measurably more balanced prototype usage.
\end{abstract}
