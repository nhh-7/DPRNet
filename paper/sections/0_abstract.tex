\begin{abstract}
Lightweight single image super-resolution (SR) seeks a favorable trade-off between
reconstruction quality and model cost for resource-constrained platforms. Content-aware
routing, which groups tokens by semantics rather than spatial position, has recently
advanced this trade-off, but its prevailing realization---the Token Aggregation Block
(TAB) of CATANet---relies on cross-batch exponential-moving-average routing centers,
discards the confidence of each token-to-center assignment, and entangles prototype
generation with confirmation. We propose \emph{Dynamic Prototype Routing} (DPR), a
drop-in replacement for TAB that keeps the backbone, the tensor interface, and the
lightweight budget unchanged. DPR generates semantic prototypes directly from the
current tokens through a soft assignment, confirms them with a learnable query bank
decoupled from generation, matches tokens back with a learnable temperature, and
propagates an explicit per-token confidence through ordering, gating, and a soft
fallback; a usage-balancing regularizer keeps prototype use spread out. The resulting
network, DPRNet, is trained on DIV2K and evaluated on five standard benchmarks at
$\times2/\times3/\times4$. DPRNet matches or slightly surpasses the strongest
content-routing baseline across benchmarks at a comparable parameter and computation
budget (e.g.\ $26.89$\,dB on Urban100 at $\times4$ with $52.14$\,G Multi-Adds, fewer than the
lightweight Transformer baselines SwinIR-light and SRFormer-light), while exposing an
interpretable, confidence-aware routing mechanism whose prototype usage is measurably more
balanced.
\end{abstract}
