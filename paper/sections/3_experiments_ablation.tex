\subsection{Ablation study}
\label{sec:ablation}

We ablate the four design elements of DPR using the independent switches exposed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:method}. All ablations are conducted at scale $\times4$, following
CATANet~\cite{liu2025catanet} whose ablations are likewise performed at $\times4$, the
setting in which content routing over high-frequency texture is most stressed. Each variant is
trained for $250$k iterations on DIV2K under the same schedule as the full model and repeated
over three seeds $\{3407,42,1234\}$. We report the mean$\pm$std across the three seeds at the
validation-best checkpoint (selected by the Set5$+$Urban100 mean, Y channel, matched
\texttt{crop\_border}). Besides PSNR/SSIM we log routing diagnostics per TAB block---the
normalized prototype-usage entropy $\mathcal{H}(u)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:balance}, the
active-prototype usage, and the learnable temperature---to inspect the routing behavior
directly. Because every variant is trained independently under the same protocol, the
differences below reflect the contribution of the switched element itself; reporting three
seeds further lets us compare not only the mean effect but also its run-to-run stability.

\paragraph{C1: dynamic prototypes vs.\ history centers.}
Table~\ref{tab:abl_c1} directly compares DPR's dynamic, per-image prototype generation
(the full model) against a cross-batch history center with an EMA update
(\emph{EMA-center}), a re-implementation of the center-based mechanism of the baseline that
keeps the identical backbone, interface, and everything downstream of the prototype
generator. Under this matched protocol, dynamic prototypes give the better
reconstruction on both benchmarks (Set5 $32.57$ vs.\ $32.51$\,dB, Urban100 $26.87$ vs.\
$26.76$\,dB) and with lower seed-to-seed variance. This provides a direct controlled
confirmation of C1: forming prototypes from the current tokens, rather than accumulating them
across batches, is what improves the routing---not merely a difference in capacity or
training budget, which are held fixed here.

\paragraph{A1: decoupled prototype confirmation.}
Table~\ref{tab:abl_a1} compares DPR with and without the query-refinement stage
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:proto_refine}). Enabling refine improves the result on both benchmarks
(Set5 $32.57$ vs.\ $32.53$\,dB; Urban100 $26.87$ vs.\ $26.82$\,dB), and because the gain
($\sim0.04$--$0.05$\,dB) exceeds the seed-to-seed standard deviation ($\sim0.02$--$0.03$\,dB),
it is attributable to the refine switch itself rather than to run-to-run noise. The refinement
is gated by a small-initialized scalar (Eq.~\eqref{eq:proto_refine}), so it acts as a residual
confirmation step that keeps the prototype slots stable while decoupling generation from
confirmation (C2); we therefore retain it by default.

\paragraph{A2: confidence-aware routing.}
Table~\ref{tab:abl_a2} adds the three confidence-aware components cumulatively:
confidence-aware ordering (Eq.~\eqref{eq:sortkey}), the IASA score gate
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:iasa_gate}), and the soft prototype fallback
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:soft_fallback}). The full configuration (v4) is clearly the best of the four on
both Set5 and Urban100, and by more than the seed standard deviation. The intermediate steps,
however, are not monotone---adding the ordering and gate alone slightly lowers PSNR before the
complete set recovers and surpasses the hard-routing baseline. We therefore read the three
switches as mutually compatible and most effective as the \emph{complete} confidence-aware
path, rather than each switch being individually accuracy-additive. We keep the components
because they make the routing path interpretable and confidence-driven: the retained per-token
confidence $x^{\mathrm{s}}$ modulates ordering, the global gate, and the fallback (C3), which
we visualize through the $x^{\mathrm{s}}$ distributions in Sec.~\ref{sec:routing_analysis}.

\paragraph{A3: prototype usage balancing.}
Table~\ref{tab:abl_a3} isolates the usage-balancing regularizer
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:balance}). Enabling it raises the block-averaged normalized usage entropy
from $0.623$ to $0.686$ on Urban100, and the increase is \emph{consistent across all
eight TAB blocks} ($b_0$--$b_7$; Table~\ref{tab:abl_a3_perblock}), i.e.\ the regularizer
drives a more balanced use of prototype slots and counteracts the collapse that the
learnable temperature would otherwise encourage (C4). Reconstruction quality is also slightly
higher with the regularizer enabled (Set5 $32.57$ vs.\ $32.55$\,dB; Urban100 $26.87$ vs.\
$26.85$\,dB), so the balancing does not trade accuracy for a flatter usage distribution.
Crucially, with three seeds available we can now assess the second part of C4: enabling the
balance loss \emph{reduces} the seed-to-seed standard deviation on both benchmarks
($0.05\!\rightarrow\!0.02$\,dB) and on the usage entropy itself
($0.008\!\rightarrow\!0.003$). The regularizer thus makes both the accuracy and the routing
behavior more stable across seeds, supporting the variance-reduction sub-claim of C4 rather
than only the balancing mechanism.

\paragraph{Summary.}
Across three seeds, the ablations show that DPR's design elements are
quality-preserving-to-positive and mutually compatible---the full model is the best variant in
every group. Beyond the mean effects, the study substantiates two mechanism claims: dynamic
prototypes outperform an EMA history center under a matched protocol (C1), and the
usage-balancing regularizer both increases prototype-usage entropy block-consistently and
lowers seed-to-seed variance (C4).
