\subsection{Model complexity and efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}

Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} compares model complexity at $\times4$, and
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_profile} gives DPRNet's per-scale profile. All DPRNet
numbers are measured under the protocol of Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_setup} (fixed
$\approx720{\times}1280$ output, thop MACs, latency over $100$ runs after $20$
warm-ups). For CATANet we additionally cite its reported $\times4$ Multi-Adds from
the original paper, measured at the same output size and therefore directly
comparable to our MACs.

\paragraph{Parameters.}
DPRNet stays within the lightweight bracket at $660$K parameters ($\times4$),
comparable to Transformer baselines such as ELAN-light ($601$K) and well below
SwinIR-light ($897$K), SRFormer-light ($873$K), and the CNN-heavy CARN
($1592$K). It is, however, larger than its direct predecessor CATANet ($535$K):
the learnable query bank and the router/score projections of DPR add parameters
relative to the EMA-center routing of CATANet. We therefore do not claim a
parameter advantage over CATANet; the comparison below is at \emph{matched
accuracy} (Urban100 $26.82$ vs.\ $26.87$, within the variance discussed in
Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\paragraph{Computation.}
In terms of Multi-Adds, DPRNet requires $45.77$\,G at $\times4$, which is in fact
slightly \emph{lower} than CATANet's $46.8$\,G at the same output resolution. Thus
the extra parameters of DPR do not translate into extra inference computation:
the dynamic prototype routing replaces CATANet's training-time EMA center updates
with per-image query refinement that is gated by a small scalar
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:proto_refine}), keeping the dominant cost---the intra-group
attention---essentially unchanged. DPRNet therefore matches CATANet-level
reconstruction at comparable compute, trading a modest parameter increase for an
interpretable, confidence-aware routing mechanism.

\paragraph{Per-scale behavior.}
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_profile} shows the expected trend across scales: for a
fixed HR output the LR feature map shrinks as the scale grows, so Multi-Adds drop
from $126.52$\,G ($\times2$) to $64.28$\,G ($\times3$) and $45.77$\,G ($\times4$),
with latency following the same order ($712.16$, $285.39$, $196.77$\,ms). The
parameter count is essentially scale-independent ($\approx0.60$--$0.67$\,M), as
only the input projection and the upsampling tail change with scale.

\paragraph{Pending measurements.}
A complete Multi-Adds and latency comparison against all eight competitors under a
single unified protocol is not yet available: published competitor compute figures
follow inconsistent parameter and measurement conventions, and latency is
hardware-dependent and cannot be borrowed from other papers' devices. We report the
quantities we can state consistently here---DPRNet's full measured profile and the
directly comparable CATANet Multi-Adds---and defer the unified competitor table to
the final version (re-measured under our protocol or taken from a single common
source). We do not fabricate the missing entries, which appear as ``-'' in
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency}.
