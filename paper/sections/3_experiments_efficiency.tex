\subsection{Model complexity and efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}

Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} compares model complexity at $\times4$, and
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_profile} gives DPRNet's per-scale profile. All DPRNet
numbers are measured under the protocol of Sec.~\ref{sec:exp_setup} with a fixed
$256{\times}256$ LR input ($\times4$ output $1024{\times}1024$), reporting thop
MACs and latency over $100$ runs after $20$ warm-ups. We deliberately adopt this
$256{\times}256$ input convention because it is the one under which the CATANet
paper reports Multi-Adds for itself, SwinIR-light, and SRFormer-light; DPRNet's
MACs are therefore directly comparable to those three cited figures.

\paragraph{Parameters.}
DPRNet stays within the lightweight bracket at $660$K parameters ($\times4$),
comparable to Transformer baselines such as ELAN-light ($601$K) and well below
SwinIR-light ($897$K), SRFormer-light ($873$K), and the CNN-heavy CARN
($1592$K). It is, however, larger than its direct predecessor CATANet ($535$K):
the learnable query bank and the router/score projections of DPR add parameters
relative to the EMA-center routing of CATANet. We therefore do not claim a
parameter advantage over CATANet; the comparison below is at \emph{matched-or-better
accuracy} (Urban100 $26.89$ vs.\ $26.87$, with the full ablation analysis in
Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}). Fig.~\ref{fig:psnr_params} plots this PSNR--parameter
trade-off at $\times4$ on Urban100: DPRNet attains the highest PSNR among the
compared lightweight methods while remaining within the same parameter bracket.

\paragraph{Computation.}
In terms of Multi-Adds, DPRNet requires $52.14$\,G at $\times4$ under the unified
$256{\times}256$ protocol. This is higher than CATANet's $46.8$\,G, so the learnable
query bank and the router/score projections of DPR do add inference cost relative to
CATANet's EMA-center routing; we therefore make no compute claim against CATANet, and
the comparison stays at \emph{matched-or-better accuracy} (Urban100 $26.89$ vs.\ $26.87$,
full ablation in Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation}). The compute story is more favorable against the
Transformer baselines: at the same input size DPRNet uses markedly fewer Multi-Adds than
both SwinIR-light ($60.3$\,G) and SRFormer-light ($56.5$\,G), while reaching the best
Urban100 PSNR in Table~\ref{tab:efficiency}. DPRNet thus remains firmly inside the
lightweight bracket, trading a modest cost increase over its direct predecessor for an
interpretable, confidence-aware routing mechanism, and a clear cost reduction relative to
attention-heavy Transformer designs at higher accuracy.

\paragraph{Per-scale behavior.}
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_profile} reports the profile at a fixed $256{\times}256$ LR
input. Because the input size is held constant, the backbone cost is shared across scales
and the Multi-Adds are dominated by the upsampling tail, so they \emph{grow} with the scale
factor---from $36.19$\,G ($\times2$) to $41.26$\,G ($\times3$) and $52.14$\,G
($\times4$)---with latency following the same order ($183.57$, $185.88$, $198.16$\,ms). The
parameter count is essentially scale-independent ($\approx0.60$--$0.67$\,M), as only the
input projection and the upsampling tail change with scale.

\paragraph{Pending measurements.}
A complete Multi-Adds and latency comparison against all eight competitors under a
single unified protocol is not yet available: the remaining competitors' published
compute figures follow inconsistent parameter and measurement conventions, and
latency is hardware-dependent and cannot be borrowed from other papers' devices. We
report the quantities we can state consistently here---DPRNet's full measured profile
and the directly comparable CATANet, SwinIR-light and SRFormer-light Multi-Adds (all
under the same $256{\times}256$ input)---and defer the unified competitor table to the
final version (re-measured under our protocol or taken from a single common source).
We do not fabricate the missing entries, which appear as ``-'' in
Table~\ref{tab:efficiency}.
