%   CATANet (46.8G) -- do NOT claim a compute win over CATANet -- but it is clearly LOWER than the

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Efficiency comparison on $\times 4$ SR. Multi-Adds are reported by thop (MACs) under a unified
fixed LR input of $256\times256$ ($\times4$ output $1024\times1024$); to compare against FLOPs-based
papers, multiply by 2. ``-'' denotes values not available under this protocol. Representative PSNR is on
Urban100 ($\times 4$).}
\label{tab:efficiency}

\begin{tabular}{l c c c c}

Method & Params (K) & Multi-Adds (G) & Latency (ms) & Urban100 PSNR \\

SwinIR-light    & 897  & 60.3  & - & 26.47 \\
ELAN-light      & 601  & -     & - & 26.54 \\
SRFormer-light  & 873  & 56.5  & - & 26.67 \\
CATANet         & 535  & \textbf{46.8} & - & \underline{26.87} \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}%
}

\vspace{2pt}
{\footnotesize
\textit{Notes.} All Multi-Adds are MACs reported by thop under a common $256\times256$ LR input
($\times4$ output $1024\times1024$): DPRNet is measured under this protocol, and the CATANet,
SwinIR-light and SRFormer-light values are taken from the CATANet paper (CVPR'25, Tab.~6), which uses
the identical input convention, so the four routing/Transformer entries are directly comparable. DPRNet
needs more Multi-Adds than its direct predecessor CATANet ($52.14$ vs.\ $46.8$\,G) but markedly fewer
than the Transformer baselines SwinIR-light ($60.3$\,G) and SRFormer-light ($56.5$\,G), while attaining
the best Urban100 PSNR. DPRNet latency is averaged over $100$ runs after $20$ warm-ups on a single CUDA
GPU. Remaining competitors' Multi-Adds (CARN/IMDN/RFDN/RLFN/ELAN-light, absent from a common source) and
all competitor latencies (device-dependent) are not available under this protocol (``-'').}
\end{table}
