Cover Letter — Neurocomputing

July 28, 2026

Dear Editor-in-Chief and Editors of Neurocomputing,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Dynamic Prototype Routing for
Lightweight Image Super-Resolution" for consideration as a research article in
Neurocomputing.

Lightweight single image super-resolution (SR) requires a careful balance between
reconstruction quality and model cost on resource-constrained platforms. Content-aware
token routing is a promising way to reach this balance, and the recent CATANet is a
strong representative: its Token Aggregation Block (TAB) groups semantically related
tokens before attention. However, the TAB routing relies on cross-batch
exponential-moving-average (EMA) centers, hard argmax assignment labels that discard
confidence, and an entangled prototype update/confirmation step. These choices limit how
quickly the router adapts to per-image content and make its behaviour hard to interpret.

In this work we propose Dynamic Prototype Routing (DPR), a drop-in replacement for the
TAB routing that keeps the CATANet backbone and its external tensor interface unchanged.
The main contributions are:

  1. Dynamic, per-image prototypes formed directly from the current tokens by a soft,
     assignment-weighted aggregation, removing the cross-batch EMA center buffer.
  2. Decoupled prototype confirmation via a learnable query bank, separating prototype
     generation from confirmation for more stable and interpretable routing slots.
  3. Confidence-aware routing: the retained per-token assignment confidence modulates
     token ordering, a global gate, and a soft prototype fallback.
  4. A learnable, bounded routing temperature together with a usage-balancing
     regularizer that prevents prototype-slot collapse.

We would like to be transparent about the scope of our claims, as we believe this is
where the contribution is best judged. On five standard benchmarks at x2/x3/x4, DPRNet
matches CATANet-level accuracy with small but consistent gains on most benchmark cells
(e.g. 26.89 dB on Urban100 at x4). DPR adds a modest cost over CATANet (660K vs 535K
parameters; 52.14G vs 46.8G Multi-Adds under a common 256x256-input protocol), so we do
not claim an efficiency advantage over CATANet; rather, DPRNet remains firmly in the
lightweight regime and uses markedly fewer Multi-Adds than the lightweight Transformer
baselines SwinIR-light (60.3G) and SRFormer-light (56.5G). The value of DPR lies in
trading this small, well-characterized cost for an interpretable, confidence-aware
routing mechanism. To support this, we report multi-seed (3-seed, mean +/- std)
ablations that (i) directly show dynamic prototypes outperform a re-implemented EMA
history-center variant under a matched protocol, and (ii) show the balancing loss makes
prototype usage both more balanced and more stable across seeds, together with routing
diagnostics (usage entropy, learned temperature, and per-token confidence distributions).

We believe this combination of a plug-and-play content-routing module, an honest
efficiency/accuracy characterization, and mechanism-level interpretability analysis fits
the scope and readership of Neurocomputing well, and complements recent lightweight
Transformer SR work published in the journal.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have read and approved the
manuscript and agree to its submission. The authors declare no competing interests. The
datasets used (DIV2K, Set5, Set14, BSD100, Urban100, Manga109) are all publicly
available; no new data were generated in this study.

We suggest the following areas of expertise for reviewers: lightweight/efficient image
super-resolution, vision Transformers, and content-aware token routing / clustering.
